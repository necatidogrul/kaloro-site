Why other trackers come in low

A photo shows the food. It does not show the oil in the pan or the butter stirred in at the end, and it cannot tell a small plate from a large one. That is why a 900-calorie meal so often gets logged as 400.

Kaloro states what it assumed — "1 plate, about 400 g, normal oil" — and gives you two taps to correct it: cooking (light, normal, generous oil) and portion (small, normal, large). The number updates as you tap.

An estimate whose assumptions are visible can be fixed. A bare number can only be wrong.

What else is in it

  • Daily calorie and macro targets from your height, weight, age and activity
  • A home screen ring showing exactly what is left today
  • Repeat — log yesterday's breakfast again with one tap
  • Weekly report: your average, your heaviest day, what you eat most
  • A widget with today's remaining calories
  • Manual entry whenever you would rather type than photograph

Home cooking, actually recognised

Most scanners are trained on restaurant plates and packaged food. Kaloro knows home cooking — including Turkish dishes, which generic models routinely misread and undercount.

Built with some restraint

Kaloro will not set a target below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 for men, will not plan faster than 0.75 kg a week, and will not accept a goal weight under a healthy BMI. Those are limits, not suggestions. Kaloro is for adults only.

Your photos stay yours

Meal photos are analysed and discarded. They are never stored on our servers. The photo lives on your phone and nowhere else.
