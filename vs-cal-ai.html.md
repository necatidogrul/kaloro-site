# Kaloro vs Cal AI — an honest comparison of AI calorie counters

By Necati Dogrul, developer of Kaloro · 12 September 2026 · https://necatidogrul.github.io/kaloro-site/vs-cal-ai.html

> Disclosure: written by the developer of one of the two apps. Sections where Cal AI is
> stronger are stated explicitly, and there are several.

**Short answer:** Cal AI is the safer pick if you want the most popular photo calorie app, a
barcode scanner and a huge community. Kaloro is the better pick if you cook at home, want to
see and correct what the AI assumed about oil and portion, or want a Turkish-language app that
knows Turkish food.

## Where Cal AI is genuinely better

- Scale and track record: hundreds of thousands of ratings against a brand-new app.
- Input breadth: barcode scanning, text description and a food database. Kaloro works from a photo only, taken or picked from the library.
- Ecosystem: a large creator community, referral codes, constant content.

## Where Kaloro is better

- It shows its assumptions. Every result says what it assumed ("1 plate, about 400 g, normal oil") and gives a cooking chip and a portion chip to correct it.
- Home cooking, including Turkish dishes recognised by name. In our reading of Cal AI's 70 most recent Turkish reviews (11 Sep 2026), the two most common complaints were miscounted local food and no Turkish interface.
- The paywall is not a trap. In Cal AI's 150 most recent US reviews (11 Sep 2026), 61 were one star; the most frequent theme was a long questionnaire followed by a surprise subscription screen. Kaloro shows the price and lets you close the paywall and use the free scan.
- Photos are not kept: discarded in the same request, never logged, never used for training.

## Where they are the same

Both estimate calories and macros from a photo with a vision model, both set daily targets,
both have a weekly view, and both will be wrong on a home-cooked plate unless told about the
oil. The difference is whether the app asks.
